1. Start with the retail plan, not the bracelet catalog

A boutique buyer can waste time comparing dozens of bracelets before deciding what the store actually needs. A better first step is to work backward from the retail plan.

Target customerGift buyer, wellness customer, fashion shopper, tourist, spiritual-store customer or another segment.
Target retail priceThe price range that makes sense beside the rest of your assortment.
Store styleMinimal, colorful, natural, spiritual, premium gift-led or trend-led.
PresentationLoose bracelet, carded product, pouch, gift box or another retail-ready format.

Auralis uses the same logic on its Boutique Wholesale page: the buyer sends the target retail price, budget, quantity, style, packaging and market before project-specific terms are confirmed.

2. Compare three wholesale routes before you ask for the lowest MOQ

Ready-to-Ship Selection

Useful when speed and market testing matter more than exclusivity. Availability, quantities and mixed-style options still need to be confirmed.

Simple Personalization

Useful when you want selected changes such as size, color balance, available accessories, brand cards or packaging without building a full collection from zero.

Custom Capsule

Useful when your boutique needs a coordinated bracelet group developed around your customer profile, target retail price, color direction and brand presentation.

Important: “Low MOQ” is not one universal number. Materials, packaging, branding, mixed styles and customization level can change what quantity is practical for a specific project.

3. What should a trial order actually test?

A trial order is useful when it answers a business question. It should help you learn which products your customers respond to, how the pricing sits in your store and whether the presentation is ready for retail.

  • Style response: Which color directions and bead scales get attention?
  • Price acceptance: Does the target retail price fit your market?
  • Presentation: Does the bracelet look complete on a card, pouch or gift package?
  • Material clarity: Can your staff or product listing describe the material accurately?
  • Reorder potential: Can the winning style be replenished or developed further?

4. Sample order vs trial order: what is the difference?

A sample is mainly for evaluating a product or design before committing to production. A trial order is broader: it tests whether the assortment works commercially in your actual store or online channel.

Sample

Check material direction, color, bead size, assembly, logo placement or packaging before approval.

Trial order

Test real customer response, merchandising, target retail price and the first reorder decision.

Repeat order

Increase the styles or quantities that proved they fit your customers and retail economics.

5. Can boutiques mix bracelet styles?

Sometimes, but it should never be assumed. Mixed styles may be possible for selected available designs, depending on current stock, total quantity and packaging method. Custom or branded projects may need different minimums.

Instead of asking only “Can I mix styles?”, send the assortment you want to test and ask which combinations are practical for the current project.

6. Decide the packaging level before requesting the quote

Packaging can change the cost, MOQ and timing. It also changes how the product performs in a boutique.

Neutral presentationUseful when the boutique already has its own merchandising or gift packaging.
Brand card or labelUseful for simple private-label presentation when project terms allow it.
Gift-ready packagingUseful when bracelets are sold as birthdays, holidays or occasion gifts.
Custom packagingUseful for a more developed retail program; setup, artwork, quantity and timing should be quoted first.

7. Material disclosure matters before you resell

Wholesale buyers need enough material information to describe the bracelet responsibly to customers. Confirm the known material direction, treatments or uncertainty, bead size, metal parts and care information for the selected assortment.

See the Material Authenticity & Disclosure Guide before writing product descriptions or sales claims.

8. What should a boutique send in the first inquiry?

You do not need a full purchasing brief. A concise retail plan is enough to start.

Business typeBoutique, gift shop, online store, wellness retailer or another channel.
Target marketCountry, customer profile and approximate retail positioning.
Target retail priceThe price range you expect the customer to pay.
Purchase budgetYour initial test budget or sample-stage budget.
Estimated quantityApproximate pieces for the first test or launch.
Style directionColor palette, reference photos, customer preferences or existing best sellers.
PackagingNeutral, branded card, pouch, box or another presentation need.
TimingDelivery country and launch or needed-by date.

Frequently asked questions

What is the MOQ for wholesale crystal bracelets?

There is no single public MOQ for every Auralis project. Minimum quantity depends on the selected design, material availability, packaging, branding and customization level, and is confirmed in the quotation.

Can a small boutique start with a trial order?

You can ask for a trial-order route in the wholesale inquiry. The practical quantity, sample terms and available styles are reviewed for the specific project before payment.

Can I add my boutique logo or custom packaging?

Brand cards, labels, message cards or custom packaging may be available depending on the project. Setup cost, artwork approval, MOQ and lead time are confirmed before production.

Can I mix several bracelet styles in one wholesale order?

Mixed styles may be possible for selected available designs, but this depends on stock, total quantity and packaging requirements and should be confirmed before quoting.

How long does a wholesale bracelet order take?

Lead time depends on materials, quantity, sampling, packaging and customization. A project-specific production and shipping estimate is confirmed with the quotation.
